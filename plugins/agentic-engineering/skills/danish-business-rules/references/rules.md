# The rules, by topic

Each row was read in the source named in [sources](sources.md), on the date given there.
"Who" says whom the rule protects: **C** consumers only, **B** business customers,
**Persons** natural persons,
**All** every visitor, recipient or customer. A section number is the act's own. Read the
section before relying on a summary.

## Cookies and other device storage

Act: the cookie order (BEK 1148 of 2011). Authority: the Agency for Digital Government.

| Rule | Who | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing is stored on, or read from, a visitor's device without consent given after adequate information | All | section 3(1) |
| The information is clear and easy to understand, says the purpose, says who stores or reads, and gives an immediate way to refuse or withdraw | All | section 3(2) |
| No consent is needed when storage only carries a transmission, or is technically required for a service the visitor expressly asked for | All | section 4 |

**Practice.** Load nothing that needs consent before the yes. Make "no" as easy as "yes".
Keep the list of what is set, by whom and why, next to the code that sets it. A cookie that
keeps a login or a basket working fits the wording of the exception. Whether anything else
does is the business's call, and the authority's guidance is to be read first.

## Marketing messages

Act: the Marketing Practices Act (consolidated act 1420 of 2024). Authority: the Consumer
Ombudsman.

| Rule | Who | Law |
|---|---|---|
| No direct marketing by electronic mail, automated calls or fax without prior consent. The person is told the consent can be withdrawn, and withdrawing is easy and free | All | section 10(1) |
| Exception: a business that got a customer's electronic address in a sale may market its own similar products to that customer, if the customer could decline, easily and free, when the address was given and in every later message | All | section 10(2) |
| Marketing by electronic mail is recognisable as marketing, says on whose behalf it is sent, and gives an address for stopping it | All | section 10(3) |
| Other channels (phone, post): not to a named person who has declined, or who is on the civil register's list of people who decline marketing | Persons | section 10(4) |
| Facts claimed in marketing can be documented | All | section 13 |

**Authority practice** (the Consumer Ombudsman's published guidance on unsolicited
messages). The ban covers every recipient: consumers, businesses and public authorities. A
trader may not ask for consent by sending an email or text message. Consent is voluntary,
informed and specific: a pre-ticked box, or consent placed in the terms, is not valid, and a
purchase may not depend on it. The consent names the business, the channel and the kinds of
product.

**Practice.** Record who consented, when, to what text and how. Put the unsubscribe link in
every message and honour it at once.

## Before a consumer orders online

Act: the Consumer Contracts Act (consolidated act 1184 of 2025). Authority: the Consumer
Ombudsman.

| Rule | Who | Law |
|---|---|---|
| A contract is a consumer contract when the customer acts mainly outside their trade. The trader has to prove that it is not | C | section 2 |
| Before a distance contract, the consumer is told clearly: the main characteristics; who the trader is, with address, phone and email; the total price including taxes and delivery costs; for subscriptions and open-ended contracts the total cost per billing period; payment and delivery terms; whether there is a right to withdraw and how to use it, or that there is none; the contract's length, how to end it and any minimum period | C | section 8(1) |
| Where the order is placed, it is stated clearly that it obliges the consumer to pay. The main characteristics, total price, cost per period, length and minimum period are shown prominently just before the order | C | section 12(1) |
| A button that places the order is labelled "ordre med betalingsforpligtelse" or similar, in an easily legible way | C | section 12(2) |
| If those two rules are not met, the consumer is not bound by the order | C | section 12(3) |
| An invitation to purchase gives the product's main characteristics, the trader's name and address, unusual payment or delivery terms, and any right to withdraw | C | Marketing Practices Act, section 6(2) |
| Prices to consumers are stated clearly, by rules set under the act | C | Marketing Practices Act, section 14 |

**Practice.** Show the full price early, not only at the last step. Put the trader's
identity on every page, not only in the terms. Send the confirmation with the same
information in an email the customer can keep.

## The right to withdraw

| Rule | Who | Law |
|---|---|---|
| A consumer may withdraw from a distance contract within 14 days without giving a reason | C | Consumer Contracts Act, sections 18(1) and 19(1) |
| The period runs from the day the contract is made for a service, and from receipt for goods | C | section 19(2) |
| There are exceptions, among them a service fully performed with the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgement that the right is lost, and goods made to the consumer's specification | C | section 18(2) |
| Where there is no right to withdraw, the consumer is told so before ordering | C | section 8(1) |
| A consumer who withdraws from a service that was started at their express request within the 14 days can be charged for the part delivered, in reasonable proportion to the whole, and only if they were told of the right to withdraw and of the amount | C | section 25(1) and (2) |
| For distance contracts made through an online interface, the consumer can also withdraw with a withdrawal function. It is labelled "Fortryd aftale" or equally clear, is available throughout the period, and is prominent and easy to reach | C | section 20 a, inserted by act 723 of 2025, in force from 19 June 2026 |

**Practice.** Relying on an exception is a decision for the business: escalate it, and
collect the consumer's express consent in the flow when the exception needs it.

## Subscriptions and other running contracts

| Rule | Who | Law |
|---|---|---|
| A consumer may end a contract for running delivery of goods or services with one month's notice to the end of a month, once five months have passed. A longer notice cannot be agreed | C | Consumer Contracts Act, section 28(1) and (5) |
| A standard-form contract says clearly with what notice, and from when, the consumer can end it | C | section 28(3) |
| Ending the contract is possible on the same medium or technical platform on which it was made | C | section 28 a |
| The act has exceptions and a longer period for some costly contracts | C | section 28(2) and (4) |

**Practice.** A subscription bought on the site is cancelled on the site, in as few steps as
buying it took. State the renewal date and price before each renewal.

## Invoices and records

| Rule | Who | Label and source |
|---|---|---|
| An invoice to another business shows: its date; a sequential number; the seller's VAT registration number; the seller's and the buyer's name and address; the quantity and nature of what was supplied; the delivery date if it differs; the price per unit without VAT and any discount; the VAT rate; and the VAT amount | B | **Authority practice**: the tax authority's legal guide, section A.B.3.3.1.4, which cites section 58(1) of the VAT order |
| Simplified invoices exist for some sales. When one is allowed, and what a private customer must be given, is not covered here | not read | not read: escalate |
| Accounting records are kept safely for five years from the end of the financial year they belong to | All | **Law**: Bookkeeping Act, section 12 |
| A consumer who is billed for a service on account gets a specified bill | C | **Law**: Marketing Practices Act, section 15 |

**Practice.** Number invoices in the system, never by hand. Do not delete an invoice: credit
it. The five years is a minimum that the privacy rules do not shorten: say so in the
retention plan.

## Accessibility

Act: the Accessibility Act for products and services (act 801 of 2022), in force from
28 June 2025.

| Rule | Who | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Services in scope may be offered only if they meet the act's accessibility requirements. Online shops and other e-commerce services for consumers are in scope | C | sections 1, 4 and 6(1) |
| A very small business that offers services is exempt from the requirements for services. Very small means fewer than ten people and at most EUR 2 million in yearly turnover or balance sheet | C | section 6(3), with the definition in section 3 |
| A requirement does not apply where it would change the service fundamentally or be a disproportionate burden. The business makes and documents that assessment | C | sections 8 and 9 |

**Practice.** Build to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines at level AA whether or not
the act applies: it is cheaper than retrofitting, and a business that grows loses the
exemption. Whether the exemption applies is the business's decision, on its own numbers.
